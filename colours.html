Understanding gauge

Lower number = thicker steel. A thicker panel gives better dent resistance and a longer service life, especially against hail and Alberta weather. Tap a gauge to see where it fits.

24 gauge — premium

Best for standing seam, high-end homes, commercial buildings, and roofs over open purlins.

The thickest of the three. It is the industry standard for standing seam and engineered commercial roofs, spans open framing better, and gives the most dent resistance against hail, heavy snow and wind.

Trade-off: Costs more than 26 gauge, is heavier and harder to work, and comes in fewer colours. More steel than most houses and outbuildings need.

Profiles: All six — 9-36, 7/8" Corrugated, 12-36, Diamond Rib, Standing Seam, Snaplock.

26 gauge — most popular for homes

The usual choice for houses and larger barns.

Noticeably stiffer than 29 gauge, so it holds up better to Alberta hail, keeps panels looking straighter, and handles snow loads well. It is the balance of strength and price most homeowners pick, and it comes in every stock colour.

Trade-off: Costs a bit more than 29 gauge.

Profiles: All six — 9-36, 7/8" Corrugated, 12-36, Diamond Rib, Standing Seam, Snaplock.

29 gauge — standard

Most common on outbuildings: sheds, garages, shops, smaller barns, and wall or liner panels.

The most economical option and plenty for buildings that are not your home. Lightweight and quick to install.

Trade-off: Dents more easily in hail and shows waviness in the panels sooner. Not the pick for a house where looks and longevity matter.

Profiles: 9-36 only.
